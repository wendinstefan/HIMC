# Email from the Chief Information Officer, Kingsmere

**From:** Priya Venkataraman, Chief Information Officer, Kingsmere University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust
**To:** Freya Lindholm (Wardline); Tom Hallworth (Deputy Chief Operating Officer, Kingsmere)
**Cc:** Ruth Castellanos (Head of Information Governance); Owen Maddox (Head of Information)
**Date:** Friday 30 October 2026, 17:52
**Subject:** RE: Horizon pilot — go-live before Christmas?

Freya, Tom,

I have read the proposal. I want this to work, and I would rather say the difficult things now than in December.

**1. My team cannot build a live interface before April 2027.** We upgrade the Corelia EPR between January and March. There is a change freeze from 14 December. Every interface analyst I have is on that upgrade. A real-time feed for Horizon is a new interface and it joins the queue behind 31 others.

**2. What you have today is a nightly file.** It leaves us at about 02:00 and covers activity up to midnight. By the 08:30 bed meeting it is eight hours old; by the 15:30 meeting it is fifteen. On a bad day the hospital changes more than that between breakfast and lunch. If the pilot runs on the nightly file, say so on the screen.

**3. The agreement we signed in March does not cover this.** It allows you to hold pseudonymised historical data for discovery and product research. Putting a forecast in front of staff who then act on it is a different purpose. Ruth will write separately. Please do not describe the data as anonymous again: it is not, and the last document that said so came back from our Caldicott Guardian with a red pen through it.

**4. Before anything informs a decision here I need:** a clinical safety case for Horizon itself under DCB0129, and our own under DCB0160; an updated DTAC pack; a penetration test that includes Horizon; a data protection impact assessment we have both signed; and a named person on your side who answers the phone when it is wrong at 3 a.m.

**5. On accuracy.** Owen's team ran their own check. Taking the last morning bed state your model had seen and saying "tomorrow will be the same" was within about nine beds on average. Your forecast was within about eight and a half. And by the time we sit down at 08:30 we also know *this* morning's number, which your nightly file does not. I am not a statistician, but I know what I would say to a finance director who asked me what the £60,000 buys. Show me where you beat us by enough to change a decision. I suspect it is admissions, not occupancy, and I suspect it is days three to seven, not tomorrow. Prove me right or wrong.

**6. Something nobody has asked us.** Half of what fills this hospital is patients who are ready to leave and cannot. That is decided by the council and the care homes, and none of it is in the data you hold. Last winter one care provider failed and we lost the equivalent of a ward for three months. Your model would have learned about it afterwards.

What I can offer: continue the nightly file under a varied agreement, if Ruth and the Caldicott Guardian approve; a "shadow" pilot from January in which the forecast is shown beside our own bed-meeting sheet and scored every day, with no decisions resting on it; and a joint review in April when my analysts come back.

That is not live before Christmas. I know that is not what your board wants to hear.

Priya
