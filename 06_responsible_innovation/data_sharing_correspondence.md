## 1. Kingsmere — what the agreement covers

**From:** Ruth Castellanos, Head of Information Governance and Data Protection Officer, Kingsmere University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust
**To:** Laszlo Varga (Wardline)
**Cc:** Priya Venkataraman; Dr Imran Sheikh (Caldicott Guardian)
**Date:** 30 September 2026
**Subject:** Horizon — data-sharing agreement and opt-outs

Laszlo,

Following the Caldicott Guardian's review of your draft impact assessment, four points.

1. **Purpose.** The agreement signed in March permits Wardline to hold a pseudonymised historical extract for "discovery and product research". It expires on 31 March 2027. It does not permit use of the data, or of anything derived from it, to inform operational decisions at this trust. Showing a forecast to 27 members of staff each morning is at the edge of that purpose. A pilot in which decisions are made is beyond it and needs a new agreement, a completed impact assessment and the Guardian's approval.

2. **Opt-outs.** Patients in England can opt out of their confidential information being used for purposes beyond their own care. About one in twenty of our patients has done so. When we checked how the June extract was produced, we found the opt-out had not been applied, because the request was logged internally as "service improvement". We are taking advice on whether it should have been. Until that is settled: do not share the extract with anyone else, do not move it outside the UK, and keep a record of every copy.

3. **Minimisation.** You hold ethnic category, deprivation decile and postcode district for 150,000 stays. Tell us what each is for. If the answer is "we might want to check the model is fair", say so, and say how; that is a legitimate purpose and we would support it. If the answer is "it was in the extract", we will send a smaller one.

4. **Staff data.** Your usage log records which named roles looked at which screen at what time. Our staff were not told. Staff-side representatives have asked what it is for.

I would like this to succeed. I would like it more if the paperwork were ahead of the product rather than behind it.

Ruth

## 2. Kisima — how the registers reached us

**Internal note by Laszlo Varga, 24 July 2026. Marked "incident — low, closed". Reopened 2 November by the Head of Responsible Innovation.**

- 14 – 16 July: two records students at Kisima photographed three months of ward admission registers on their own phones, at the Medical Superintendent's request, to help us.
- 16 July: 380 photographs were sent by WhatsApp to our researcher's personal phone. The pages showed patients' names, inpatient numbers, ages, villages, diagnoses and next-of-kin telephone numbers. Some diagnoses were written in the abbreviations the wards use for conditions that carry stigma.
- 17 – 22 July: our researcher forwarded the photographs to a personal email account and then to a contractor, who typed them into a spreadsheet. The contractor removed names, numbers and telephone numbers. Diagnoses, ages and villages were kept.
- 23 July: I became aware. There was no data-sharing agreement, no assessment under Kenya's Data Protection Act, and no record of what the hospital's own ethics committee would require. The photographs had left Kenya.
- 24 July: photographs deleted from the researcher's phone, the email account and the contractor's laptop. I cannot confirm deletion from WhatsApp backups or from the students' phones.
- The typed file (`inpatient_register_sample_apr_jun_2026.csv`) is still in use.

*Questions I did not answer in July: Was the hospital entitled to share this? Were we entitled to receive it? Should the typed file exist? Do we owe anyone a notification? The Medical Superintendent acted in good faith and so did our researcher. That is not the same as it being all right.*
