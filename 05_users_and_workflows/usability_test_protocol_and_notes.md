# Usability test — protocol and observer notes

Results are in `usability_test_participants.csv` (14 people) and `usability_test_results.csv` (84 task attempts). The screens tested are described in `07_prototype_v0_3/prototype_screens.md`.

## Protocol

- **Prototype:** Horizon v0.3, clickable, loaded with Kingsmere data for the week beginning Monday 19 October 2026. Kenyan and Indonesian participants saw the same Kingsmere screens: no local version exists.
- **Sessions:** 30 minutes, one participant at a time, thinking aloud. UK sessions in person on the participant's usual device. Kenya and Indonesia by video call; Indonesia through an interpreter.
- **Dates:** 20 – 22 October (UK), 27 October (Kenya), 28 October (Indonesia).
- **Recruitment:** UK participants were suggested by the Deputy Chief Operating Officer. Kenyan and Indonesian participants were suggested by each hospital's management.
- **No training** was given beforehand. Participants were told: "This shows a forecast for the next seven days. It may be wrong."

### Tasks

| Task | Instruction | Counts as completed if |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Find the forecast number of adult beds needed this Thursday | States the central number for Thursday |
| T2 | Say in your own words how sure the tool is about Thursday's number | Explains that the true number is expected to fall inside the range about four times in five, or equivalent |
| T3 | Decide whether you would open the escalation ward for the weekend, and say why | Makes a decision and refers to the forecast *and* its range |
| T4 | Find which part of the hospital is driving the pressure | Reaches the ward-group breakdown and names a group |
| T5 | Record that you disagree with Thursday's forecast and give a reason | Submits the override form with a reason |
| T6 | Find out how wrong the forecast was last week | Reaches the accuracy page and states an error figure correctly |

After the tasks each participant completed the ten-item System Usability Scale (SUS, scored 0 – 100; 68 is the usual benchmark for "average").

## Observer notes

**The range.** Eight of fourteen participants read the shaded band as the lowest and highest the number could possibly be. One site manager, shown "435 (426 – 444)" for Thursday, said: "So worst case 444. I can live with 444." Two ignored the band entirely.

**Colour beats numbers.** The home screen colours each day green, amber or red. Three participants made the escalation decision from the colour alone. Nobody asked how the colour thresholds were set.

**"Beds needed" or "beds occupied"?** The headline says "beds needed". The number underneath is a forecast of beds *occupied*. One participant spotted the difference. He was the information analyst.

**The override form.** Six of fourteen could not complete this task and four more needed help. Three found the form and declined to give a reason: "Who reads this?"

**The accuracy page** is three clicks from the home screen. Two participants found it unaided.

**Wards.** Ward managers looked for their own ward and found "medicine" and "surgery".

**Phones.** On a mid-range Android phone over mobile data (Kisima) the home screen took 40 – 50 seconds to load and the chart did not fit the screen. One participant ended the session early to save her data bundle.

**Language.** The Indonesian head nurse did not recognise "occupancy"; her term is BOR. The interpreter had no settled translation for "prediction interval".

**What they asked for, unprompted:**

- "Where do I tell it Ward 5 is shut?" (site manager)
- "Show me yesterday: what you said and what happened." (four participants)
- "Patients, nurses and oxygen, not beds." (Medical Superintendent, Kisima)
- "By room and by class." (deputy director, Sukamaju)
- "Put it in the bed meeting sheet. Don't make me log in." (site manager, nights)

## Limitations the researcher noted

- Fourteen people. Every UK participant was chosen by the sponsor.
- No healthcare assistants, no staff nurses, no doctors below consultant, no patients.
- A test in a quiet room is not a bed meeting at OPEL 4.
- Kenyan and Indonesian participants were shown a British hospital's data.
