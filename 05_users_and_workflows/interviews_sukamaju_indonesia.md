# Interview notes — RSUD Sukamaju (Indonesia)

*Four conversations by video call with an interpreter, September – October 2026. Notes by Wardline's product researcher. Quotations are the interpreter's English rendering.*

---

## S1. Wakil Direktur Pelayanan (deputy director, services), 5 years in role — 9 September

- Since the room changes in July 2025 the hospital has 34 fewer beds and the same number of patients. "Every morning I count who is waiting in the emergency department. Before, it was nobody."
- Wants the forecast in the morning report: by ward, by class, for tomorrow and for the week.
- "A total for the hospital is not useful. I can have twenty empty beds and no bed for a Class 3 man with tuberculosis."
- Dengue: "In February the children's ward had forty patients in thirty-two beds. We knew it was coming. We did not know how many."
- The insurer pays a fixed amount per case. "A forecast that helps me send patients home one day earlier is worth money to me. One that only tells me I am full is not."
- Asked who would act on the forecast: the admissions desk and the head nurses. Asked whether they had been consulted: "Not yet."

## S2. Kepala ruangan (head nurse), Mawar internal medicine ward, 10 years in role — 28 October

- 40 beds since the change, down from 48. Usually full.
- "What is 'occupancy'? We say BOR. And is the range a mistake? If you do not know the number, why show two?"
- Decides every day which patient moves to make room. "The computer does not know that the family of bed 12 has not arrived to take him home."
- Uses WhatsApp on her own phone for everything. The ward computer is for the doctors' orders and billing.
- Would look at a forecast "if it is in the morning report. I will not open another application."

## S3. SIMRS officer (hospital IT), 7 years in role — 28 October

- "Technically we can send a file each night. An interface needs the vendor, and the vendor will ask who is paying."
- The server was down for three days in September 2025. The census for those days was typed in afterwards from paper.
- The label for the insurer changed in the system on 1 July 2025 ("BPJS" became "JKN"). "It is the same thing. Your analysts asked me if a new insurer had arrived."
- Patient data must stay in Indonesia, as he understands the hospital's contract with the vendor and the regency's rules. Has not seen a legal opinion.

## S4. Sales director, PT Nusa Medika Digital (the SIMRS vendor) — 30 September

- The company's system runs in about 140 public hospitals.
- Proposal: Wardline's forecast becomes a module in the vendor's system, under the vendor's name. The vendor keeps 40% of revenue and has exclusive rights in Indonesia for five years. The model runs on the vendor's servers in Jakarta.
- "Public hospitals buy from the e-catalogue, from companies they know. You are not in the catalogue. We are."
- Price expectation for a Type B public hospital: "Perhaps two hundred to three hundred million rupiah a year." (About £10,000 – £14,000.)
- On the private hospital group that has also approached Wardline: "Exclusive means exclusive."
- Wants an answer by the end of November.
