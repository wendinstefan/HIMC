# Interview notes — Kisima County Referral Hospital (Kenya)

*Four conversations, August – October 2026: two in person during the August visit, two by video call. Notes by Wardline's product researcher. Words in quotation marks are verbatim.*

---

## N1. Medical Superintendent, 6 years in role — 11 August (in person)

- "You keep saying beds. A bed here is a mattress. I can put two children on it. What I cannot do is make a nurse, or oxygen, or a unit of blood."
- Knows roughly when the bad weeks will come: about two months after the heavy rain. Does not know how bad.
- "A week ahead is short for me. To get locum nurses I write to the county and wait a month. Tell me in May how bad June will be."
- Seven days would still help with three things: calling in off-duty staff, borrowing oxygen cylinders, and warning the county finance office.
- On strikes: "No model predicts those. I learn from the radio like everyone else. When it comes, the wards empty in three days."
- On the Foundation's money: "It is welcome. But I have seen four pilots. Each one left a dashboard and a password nobody remembers."
- Asked what she would need in order to trust a forecast: "Show me last June. What would you have told me in May?"

## N2. Nursing officer in charge, paediatric ward, 12 years in role — 12 August (in person)

- 60 official beds. On one night in June she had 142 children. Two nurses on nights.
- Fills in the bed-state book at about five in the morning. "After twelve hours my additions are not always correct. I know this."
- Would a forecast help? "If it brings me a third nurse, yes. If it only tells me I will be full, I know that already. It is raining."
- Uses her own phone and her own data bundle for the ward WhatsApp group. Would not want an app that uses much data.
- Worries that numbers sent to the county will be used to blame the ward for deaths.

## N3. Health Records & Information Officer, 9 years in role — 27 October (video)

- Compiles the monthly report for every ward by hand from the registers. Two staff for the whole hospital.
- "The monthly figures are good for the Ministry. For running the hospital tomorrow they are history."
- Could type each ward's midnight count into a phone every morning "if it takes five minutes and if it replaces something. If it is one more register, no."
- During the strike nobody filled in the books. "You will have a hole in your data exactly when the most interesting thing happened."
- On the photographed registers we were sent: "The students removed the names. The diagnoses are still there, and the village, and the age. In a small place people can work out who that is."

## N4. Programme lead, Afya Bora Foundation — 20 October (video)

- The Foundation funds digital tools for county health departments in three countries.
- Offer: USD 150,000 over two years for a Kisima pilot, extending to two more counties if it goes well.
- Conditions: the tool is free to counties for three years; the Foundation receives the hospital-level data for its own research and publications; the Foundation's name comes first.
- "Our board likes things that scale. Fourteen counties already use our dashboards."
- Asked who maintains the tool after the grant: "That would be for the county to budget for."
- Asked whether the county or the patients had been consulted on the data-sharing condition: "It is standard in our agreements."
